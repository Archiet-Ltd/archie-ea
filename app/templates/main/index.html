The category

Every enterprise runs on knowledge it cannot see in one place.

Every company, from a five-person startup to a global group, has a strategy, the capabilities that deliver it, the systems and suppliers those capabilities depend on, the money it spends, the risks it carries and the people who own each piece. That knowledge is spread across spreadsheets, diagrams, contracts, tickets and people's heads. When someone asks "what breaks if this fails?", "what does this really cost?" or "are we doing what we said we would?", the answer is assembled by hand, late, and rarely trusted. An AI assistant asked the same question, with no grounded source to check, guesses — confidently and wrongly.

This isn't a search problem, and it isn't a chatbot problem. Searching your documents faster doesn't fix knowledge that was never written down in a structured, checkable form in the first place, and a chatbot trained on whatever it can find repeats the same gaps with more confidence. What's missing is a living, explainable model of how the enterprise actually works — its strategy, systems, data, people, money, risk and suppliers — kept current, and built so both the people who run the business and the AI assistants working alongside them have one governed source to ask instead of guessing.

Enterprise Intelligence Management is the capability that builds that model. Customer relationship management gave every company one record of its customers. Enterprise resource planning gave it one record of its operations and finances. Enterprise Intelligence Management gives it one record of the enterprise itself: a company brain holding strategy, business, applications, data, technology, people, money, risk, suppliers, operations and AI, kept current from the systems that master each fact, and turned into answers, insights, recommendations and simulations for every person who runs the business, and for the machines that work for them.

It is a capability, not a department. A startup is an enterprise. So is a 200-person family business, a scale-up preparing for its first audit and a multinational with ten thousand applications. Each of them needs to know what it runs on, what that costs, what could go wrong and whether its plans are working. What changes with size is the depth of the model, not the need for it.

AI assistants

Give your AI assistants one true source about your company.

Your teams already ask AI assistants about your systems, suppliers, risks and plans. Without a source of truth, those assistants guess. A company brain closes that loop: Entelim connects to AI assistants through the Model Context Protocol, the open standard for linking assistants to tools and data, so an assistant asks the same governed questions the platform's own screens ask and gets back answers that cite the records they came from.

  • You connect it yourself. Any member can connect their own AI assistant from their account, and an administrator can disconnect it at any time.
  • Read first, change only with approval. Assistants start with read access; a suggested change arrives in your approval queue, and nothing changes until a person approves it.
  • Every call on the record. Each request is logged with the assistant, the grant and the person it acted for.

Connecting your own AI assistant is live on every plan. Entelim's own AI chat works the same way: every action passes one permission check and every change waits for approval.
