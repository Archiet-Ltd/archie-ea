# COBIT 2019 in Entelim: 40 objectives on your estate

COBIT 2019 is ISACA's framework for the governance and management of enterprise information and technology. It sets out 40 objectives in five domains: Evaluate, Direct and Monitor for governance, and Align, Plan and Organize; Build, Acquire and Implement; Deliver, Service and Support; and Monitor, Evaluate and Assess for management. It is guidance that organisations tailor, and a tool cannot certify it. Entelim holds the objectives and the estate they concern in one place.

## Who uses COBIT 2019, and when

COBIT 2019 suits organisations where the board or an auditor asks how technology is governed: who decides, who benefits, who bears the risk. It is used by chief information officers, internal auditors and risk managers, often in regulated industries. It is wide, and most organisations choose a handful of objectives that match their concerns. Entelim helps by holding the records those objectives rely on, such as the portfolio, business cases, risks and architecture, in one linked model rather than in separate reports.

## How Entelim loads COBIT 2019 at sign-up

Tick COBIT 2019 on the company step of sign-up, or adopt it later in Settings, and Entelim adopts the framework with its own list of the 40 objectives, from EDM01 to MEA04, each in its domain. They appear on the Frameworks page and the compliance dashboard. Nothing is recorded as achieved when you adopt it.

## What you get

- All 40 COBIT 2019 objectives, in their five domains.
- A portfolio and business cases for the benefits and resource objectives.
- A risk register for the risk objectives, and an architecture review board for governance.
- An enterprise architecture model, which is what the 'managed enterprise architecture' objective asks you to keep.

## How to use it

1. Choose COBIT 2019 at sign-up, or adopt it from Settings.
2. Read the objectives on the compliance dashboard and pick those your board cares about.
3. For portfolio and budget objectives, register initiatives and attach business cases with costs and benefits.
4. For risk objectives, record risks and link them to the applications they would affect.
5. For enterprise architecture, build the model and bring decisions to the review board.

## Common mistakes

The usual mistakes are trying to implement all 40 objectives and measuring conformance rather than whether technology serves the business. Choose the objectives that match your concerns, assign an owner to each, and report on outcomes. Treat the framework as a vocabulary for the board conversation, not a project in itself. Review the chosen objectives once a year, drop those that no longer matter, and add the ones the business now asks about.
