# TOGAF in Entelim: the ADM phases on a live model

TOGAF is an enterprise architecture framework from The Open Group. Its core is the Architecture Development Method, a cycle of phases that takes an organisation from a vision of change, through business, information systems and technology architecture, to planning, governance and managing change. It is a method, not a tool, and most teams adapt it. Entelim holds the phases as a list and puts the model they describe next to it.

## Who uses TOGAF, and when

TOGAF suits organisations with an architecture function, or a change large enough to need one: a merger, a platform replacement, a move to the cloud. Architects use it to agree a common method and vocabulary, and to show a board how a decision was reached. It is deliberately flexible; the specification expects each organisation to tailor it. A small team might use only the vision, the target architecture and the migration plan, and that is a legitimate use. Entelim keeps the artefacts those phases produce in one model so they stay current.

## How Entelim loads TOGAF at sign-up

Tick TOGAF on the company step of sign-up, or adopt it later in Settings, and Entelim adopts the framework with its own list: the Preliminary phase, phases A to H and Requirements Management, each with a short description of what it is for. They appear on the Frameworks page and the compliance dashboard. The model underneath is ArchiMate-based, so what you draw in the business, application and technology layers is the architecture TOGAF asks you to describe.

## What you get

- The ADM phases as a list: Preliminary, Architecture Vision, Business, Information Systems, Technology, Opportunities and Solutions, Migration Planning, Implementation Governance, Architecture Change Management and Requirements Management.
- An architecture model in ArchiMate, with diagrams drawn from the elements in it.
- Roadmaps and a portfolio for migration planning, and an architecture review board for implementation governance.
- AI chat answers that cite the model elements they used.

## How to use it

1. Choose TOGAF at sign-up, or adopt it from Settings.
2. Read the phases on the compliance dashboard and decide which your change needs; TOGAF expects you to tailor it.
3. For phases B to D, model the business, application and technology architecture as elements, and draw diagrams from them.
4. For phases E and F, record the work packages and place them on a roadmap.
5. For phase G, submit changes to the architecture review board and record the decision and its conditions.

## Common mistakes

The usual mistakes are following the method as a ritual, producing documents nobody reads, and letting the architecture repository drift from reality. Prefer a small number of artefacts that are kept current over a complete set that is not. Treat the phases as questions to ask, and stop when you have the answer you need.
