# NIST CSF in Entelim: six functions on your model

The NIST Cybersecurity Framework is guidance from the US National Institute of Standards and Technology for managing cybersecurity risk. Version 2.0 organises it into six functions: Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond and Recover, each divided into categories. It is a voluntary framework, not a certification, and an organisation uses it to describe where it is and where it wants to be. Entelim holds the structure so you can work through it beside your own estate.

## Who uses NIST CSF, and when

The NIST Cybersecurity Framework suits organisations that want a plain way to describe their security programme to a board, a regulator or a customer, without committing to an audit. It is widely used in the United States and by suppliers to US customers, and it is a common reference when a programme has grown without an overall structure. The framework starts with Govern and Identify: decide who is accountable, then find out what you have. Entelim supports both, because the roles and the inventory live in the model.

## How Entelim loads NIST CSF at sign-up

Choose NIST CSF on the company step of sign-up, or adopt it later in Settings, and Entelim adopts the framework with its own list: the Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond and Recover functions and the categories under each, such as asset management, risk assessment, data security and incident management. The list appears on the Frameworks page and the compliance dashboard. Adopting it records nothing as achieved.

## What you get

- The six functions and their categories as a list you can work through.
- A model of your applications, owners and vendors for the Identify function to be read against.
- A risk register linked to the systems each risk would reach, for risk assessment and supply chain categories.
- AI chat answers that cite the records they came from.

## How to use it

1. Tick NIST CSF at sign-up, or adopt it from Settings.
2. Open the compliance dashboard and read the functions and categories.
3. Start with Identify: check every application has an owner and a vendor, since asset management begins with knowing what you have.
4. Record risks in the risk register and link each to the applications it would affect.
5. Ask the AI chat which applications have no owner, and open the records behind the answer.

## Common mistakes

The usual mistakes are scoring yourself against the categories without evidence, and treating Govern as paperwork. Decide who is accountable for each function, write down how you know, and revisit it. The framework is useful because it makes gaps visible, not because it grades you.
