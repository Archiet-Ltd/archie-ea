# ITIL 4 in Entelim: 34 practices beside your services

ITIL 4 is a body of guidance on IT service management from AXELOS, now part of PeopleCert. It describes 34 management practices in three groups: general management, service management and technical management, from incident management and change enablement to supplier management and service configuration management. It is guidance, and an organisation adopts the parts it needs. Entelim holds the practices as a list next to the model of your services.

## Who uses ITIL 4, and when

ITIL 4 suits an IT department that runs services for the business and wants a common language for how they are managed: how requests are handled, how changes are approved, how incidents become problems. It is widely used in operations, service desks and IT management, and many organisations adopt a handful of practices rather than all 34. Your service desk keeps running the tickets. Entelim holds the thing the practices manage: the applications and services, their owners, their suppliers and their lifecycle.

## How Entelim loads ITIL 4 at sign-up

Choose ITIL 4 on the company step of sign-up, or adopt it later in Settings, and Entelim adopts the framework with its own list of all 34 practices, grouped as general, service and technical management practices. They appear on the Frameworks page and the compliance dashboard. Adopting records nothing as done.

## What you get

- All 34 ITIL 4 practices, grouped, in a list you can scan.
- An application inventory with owners, vendors and lifecycle stage, which supports service configuration and asset practices.
- A vendor and contract register for supplier management.
- A risk register and an architecture review board for risk and change practices.

## How to use it

1. Tick ITIL 4 at sign-up, or adopt it from Settings.
2. Read the practices and mark the handful that matter to you now.
3. For service configuration, open the application inventory and give every application a named owner and a lifecycle stage.
4. For supplier management, enter your vendors and their contracts in the registers.
5. For risk management, log risks and link each to the services it would reach.

## Common mistakes

The usual mistakes are adopting all 34 practices at once and measuring activity instead of outcomes. Start with the practices where the business feels pain, such as incident management or change enablement, and improve those before moving on. Keep the service records accurate; every other practice depends on them. A practice is worth adopting when someone owns it, when you can say what good looks like for it, and when you will notice if it slips.

## Why Entelim is different

ITIL practices are easier to describe than to keep honest, because the service records go out of date. Entelim keeps the estate as a model, so a practice such as service configuration management has real records under it. Connect GitHub or Jira at sign-up and the inventory starts from what you already run. The AI chat cites the records behind each answer.
