# Ansoff Matrix: four growth routes, compared

The Ansoff Matrix, devised by Igor Ansoff, sorts growth strategies by two choices: whether the products are existing or new, and whether the markets are existing or new. That gives four routes: market penetration, product development, market development and diversification, with risk rising from the first to the last. It helps a leadership team say which kind of growth it is pursuing and what that asks of it.

## Who uses the Ansoff Matrix, and when

The Ansoff Matrix suits a leadership team choosing where growth will come from and wanting to be explicit about the risk. Selling more of the same to the same customers is the safest route; a new product in a new market is the riskiest. Teams use it to avoid a portfolio of growth ideas that all sit in the riskiest corner, or all in the safest. It pairs naturally with a business case for the chosen route.

## How to use it

1. Open Canvases from the sidebar and choose Ansoff Matrix.
2. Write what you could do in each of the four boxes, as specific moves rather than themes.
3. Link each move to the capabilities and applications it would need.
4. Mark which moves are already under way and which are options.
5. Take the chosen move to a business case, with its costs and expected benefits.
