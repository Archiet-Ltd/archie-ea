# ISO 27001 in Entelim: Annex A controls on your model

ISO/IEC 27001 is the international standard for running an information security management system: a set of requirements for deciding which information risks matter to an organisation, and Annex A, a list of controls to treat them. An organisation is assessed against it by an independent auditor; certification belongs to the auditor. Entelim keeps the standard's controls next to the things they are about, so the people preparing for an audit are working from the same model as everyone else.

## Who uses ISO 27001, and when

ISO 27001 suits a company that sells to customers who ask for evidence of how it protects information, or that wants a recognised structure for its security work. It is common among software and services firms, and among suppliers to banks, healthcare and government. A small team can begin without a consultant: the first job is not writing policies but knowing which systems hold information that matters, who owns them and which suppliers touch them. That inventory is what an auditor will ask for first, and it is what Entelim builds from your repositories and projects. The standard then asks you to decide which risks you will treat, and to show that you did.

## How Entelim loads ISO 27001 at sign-up

Tick ISO 27001 on the company step of sign-up, or later in Settings, and Entelim adopts the ISO 27001 catalogue for your organisation. The controls it holds are listed with their code, title and group. ISO 27001:2022 Annex A defines 93 controls, and the framework screen states how many of them are loaded, as in the screenshot above, where the ISO 27001 row shows its loaded controls against the 93. Nothing is marked as met when you adopt it; every control starts as work to look at.

## What you get

- The ISO 27001 controls in a list you can filter and work through, shown on the Frameworks page and on the compliance dashboard.
- A count of the controls loaded against the 93 in Annex A, shown on the framework screen.
- Your applications, owners, vendors and risks in the same model, so a control about suppliers or asset ownership can be read against real records.
- Answers in the AI chat that cite the records they came from, which an auditor can open and check.

## How to use it

1. Choose ISO 27001 on the company step of sign-up, or adopt it later from Settings.
2. Open the compliance dashboard and read the controls that Entelim lists for it.
3. For each control that concerns assets, ownership or suppliers, open the application, vendor or risk records it touches and check they are filled in.
4. Record the risks you find in the risk register so they sit in the model rather than in a spreadsheet.
5. Ask the AI chat which applications have no owner or which vendors hold no contract, and open the records behind each answer.

## Common mistakes

The usual mistakes are writing policies before knowing the systems they cover, setting a scope so wide that nobody can evidence it, and treating the list of Annex A controls as a checklist to tick. The standard asks you to choose controls because of your risks, and to be able to say why you left one out. Entelim helps you keep the reasons next to the risks that prompted them.

## Why Entelim is different

A security standard usually lives in a spreadsheet that nobody keeps in step with the estate. In Entelim the controls sit on the same model as your applications, vendors and risks, so the evidence you look for is a record you can open. Connect GitHub or Jira at sign-up and the model starts from the repositories and projects you already have, rather than from a blank page. Every answer the AI chat gives cites the records behind it.
