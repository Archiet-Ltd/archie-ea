# Blue Ocean strategy in Entelim: four actions, one canvas

Blue Ocean strategy, from W. Chan Kim and Renée Mauborgne, argues for creating uncontested market space instead of fighting over existing demand. Its working tool lists the factors an industry competes on, then asks four questions: which factors to eliminate, which to reduce, which to raise and which to create. The result is a value curve that looks different from competitors'.

## Who uses the Blue Ocean, and when

Blue Ocean suits a company in a crowded market that competes on the same factors as everyone else and is tired of it. It also suits a new entrant that cannot win on the incumbent's terms. The exercise is to list what the industry competes on, then challenge each factor. The risk is wishful thinking, so test each proposed change with customers before treating it as a strategy.

## How to use it

1. Open Canvases from the sidebar and choose Blue Ocean.
2. List the factors your industry competes on, such as price, speed, accuracy or service.
3. Decide which factors to eliminate that the industry takes for granted.
4. Decide what to reduce well below the industry standard, and what to raise well above it.
5. List what to create that the industry has never offered, and take the strongest to a business case.
