# GDPR in Entelim: data protection beside your systems

The General Data Protection Regulation is the European Union law on how personal data is collected, used and protected. It applies to any organisation that handles the personal data of people in the EU, and it is enforced by regulators, not by certificate. Entelim does not give legal advice and cannot make an organisation compliant. It helps with a practical first question: which of our systems hold personal data, who owns them, and which suppliers touch them.

## Who uses GDPR, and when

GDPR matters to any organisation that handles personal data about people in the European Union, whatever its own size or location. A company usually meets it through a customer's data-processing questions, a subject access request or a change of supplier. The practical starting point is the same each time: a list of the systems that hold personal data, who is responsible for each, and which suppliers process it for you. Entelim keeps that list in the application inventory and the vendor and contract registers, so it is one query away when someone asks.

## How Entelim loads GDPR at sign-up

Tick GDPR on the company step of sign-up, or adopt it later in Settings, and Entelim adds the GDPR catalogue to your organisation. The articles and requirements it holds are listed with a code, a title and a group on the Frameworks page and the compliance dashboard. Nothing is marked as met when you adopt it.

## What you get

- The GDPR requirements as a list, shown beside any other framework you work with.
- An application inventory in which each application has an owner, a vendor and a lifecycle stage.
- Vendors and contracts in registers, so processors you rely on are recorded in one place.
- A risk register that links each risk to the systems it affects, and AI chat answers that cite their records.

## How to use it

1. Choose GDPR at sign-up, or adopt it from Settings.
2. Read the requirements in the catalogue on the compliance dashboard.
3. List the applications that process personal data and give each a named owner.
4. Record the vendors that process data for you, with their contracts, in the vendor and contract registers.
5. Add the risks to personal data that you have identified, and link each to the applications it would reach.

## Common mistakes

The usual mistakes are assuming personal data lives only in the obvious systems, and forgetting suppliers who process it on your behalf. Spreadsheets, exports and analytics tools often hold more than the main database. Keep the list of systems current, and review it when a new tool is bought.

## Why Entelim is different

Questions about personal data are questions about the estate: which application, which supplier, which owner. Entelim keeps that estate as one model, so the answer to 'where do we hold this data?' is a record, not a recollection. Connect GitHub or Jira at sign-up and the model starts from the services and projects you already have. Each answer the AI chat gives cites the records behind it, so you can open them and check.
