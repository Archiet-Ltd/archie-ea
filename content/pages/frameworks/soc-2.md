# SOC 2: trust criteria next to your systems

SOC 2 is an attestation report, written by an independent auditor, on how a service organisation controls security and, where in scope, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality and privacy. The criteria come from the American Institute of Certified Public Accountants. A company is examined by an independent auditor, who writes the report. Entelim helps with the part before that: knowing what you run, who owns it and which risks are recorded against it.

## Who uses SOC 2, and when

SOC 2 suits a company whose customers, usually in North America, ask for a report before they sign. A start-up often meets it when its first large customer sends a security questionnaire. The scope is yours to define: you choose which systems and which trust criteria the auditor examines. Deciding that scope is easier when the list of systems is current and each has an owner. Entelim keeps that list, and the vendors and contracts behind it, so the conversation with an auditor begins from records instead of from a search through shared drives.

## How Entelim loads SOC 2 at sign-up

Choose SOC 2 on the company step of sign-up, or adopt it later in Settings, and Entelim adds the SOC 2 catalogue to your organisation. Its criteria are listed with a code, a title and a group, on the Frameworks page and on the compliance dashboard. Adopting a framework changes no record of yours and marks nothing as satisfied.

## What you get

- The SOC 2 criteria as a list you can filter, beside any other framework you have adopted.
- Applications, vendors, contracts and risks in the same model, so a criterion about change management or vendor oversight points at records that exist.
- A risk register in which each risk can be linked to the systems it would reach.
- AI chat answers that cite the records they were built from.

## How to use it

1. Tick SOC 2 at sign-up, or adopt it from Settings.
2. Open the compliance dashboard and read the criteria in the catalogue.
3. Check that each in-scope application has a named owner and a recorded vendor, because auditors ask who is responsible for a system.
4. Record the contracts of the vendors your service relies on, so supplier oversight can be shown from one place.
5. Log the risks you accept or treat in the risk register, and link each to the systems it affects.

## Common mistakes

The usual mistakes are including more systems and criteria than the customer needs, and collecting evidence in a rush in the weeks before the examination. An auditor expects controls to have operated over time, so records that are kept as you work are worth far more than a folder assembled at the end.
