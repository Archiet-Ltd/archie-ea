# Empathy Map: see your customer's view

An Empathy Map, popularised by Dave Gray, puts one person at the centre and records what they say, think, do and feel, and then their pains and gains. It is used before design work to replace assumptions about a customer, user or stakeholder with observations. It describes one person or one type of person, not a market.

## Who uses the Empathy Map, and when

An Empathy Map suits a team that is about to design something for someone and realises it has been guessing. It is used in product discovery, service design and change programmes, to understand a customer, a user or a stakeholder whose support is needed. It is done best with evidence in hand, from interviews, support requests or observation, and in a group. Gaps in the map are as useful as entries, because they show what you do not know.

## How to use it

1. Open Canvases from the sidebar and choose Empathy Map.
2. Name the user at the centre: a role or a type of customer, not a whole market.
3. Fill says and does from interviews and observation first; they are the things you can quote or see.
4. Add what the person thinks and feels, marking inferences as inferences.
5. Finish with pains and gains, and carry the strongest of each into your value proposition.
