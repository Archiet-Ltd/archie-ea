# Wardley Map in Entelim: map needs against evolution

A Wardley Map, from Simon Wardley, places the components of a service on two axes: how visible they are to the user, and how far they have evolved, from genesis through custom-built and product to commodity. It shows where a team is building what it could buy and what is about to become a utility. It is used to challenge strategy rather than to describe it.

## Who uses the Wardley Map, and when

Wardley Mapping suits architects, product leaders and technology executives who must decide what to build, buy or rent, and want to see how the landscape is changing. It is especially useful for spotting a custom-built component that has become a commodity, or a commodity that is being treated as a differentiator. It takes practice, and a rough map made quickly is better than an exact one made never.

## How Entelim opens the Wardley Map at sign-up

The boxes are the user, the user need, genesis, custom built, product, commodity, movement and inertia. A canvas that exists is left as you have it, so choosing it again never overwrites your work.

## What you get

- The Wardley Map with its boxes (the user, the user need, genesis, custom built, product, commodity, movement and inertia), each saved on its own as you work.
- Boxes that take elements from your model, so you choose what already exists and name only what is new.
- One model beneath all your canvases: an element used here appears wherever else it belongs, and is changed once.
- An export of the canvas as an image, for the person who asks to see it.
- Where AI is enabled, a draft button for a single box. The draft is advice only: nothing is written until you edit and save it.

## How to use it

1. Open Canvases from the sidebar and choose Wardley Map.
2. Name the user and their need at the top of the map.
3. List the components that meet the need and place each by how evolved it is.
4. Record movement, where a component is shifting, and inertia, what resists the shift.
5. Question anything custom-built that has become a product or commodity, and take the decision to the review board.
