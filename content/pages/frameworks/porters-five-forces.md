# Porter's Five Forces in Entelim: competition on one page

Porter's Five Forces, from Michael Porter, assesses the structure of an industry through five pressures on profit: the threat of new entrants, the bargaining power of suppliers, the bargaining power of buyers, the threat of substitutes and rivalry among existing competitors. It shows why an industry earns what it earns, and where a company could shift its position.

## Who uses the Porter's Five Forces, and when

Porter's Five Forces suits a company asking whether an industry is worth entering, or why its margins are under pressure. Strategy teams, investors and founders use it. The analysis is only as good as the industry boundary you draw, so define it first. A narrow boundary shows more rivalry, a wide one hides it. Check each force against evidence such as how concentrated your buyers are, rather than against a feeling.

## How to use it

1. Open Canvases from the sidebar and choose Porter's Five Forces.
2. Define the industry you are assessing before anything else; the forces change with the boundary.
3. Assess each force with specific evidence: who the rivals are, how many buyers there are, what a switch costs.
4. Link suppliers and competitors to the vendors and organisations already in the model.
5. Decide which force is strongest and which of your choices could reduce it.
